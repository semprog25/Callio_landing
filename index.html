Privacy

Your calls stay yours.

Callio is designed around local-first call data. Authentication is separate from your private call history.

Local-first call data

Call history and Rescue context are designed to stay on your device.

Sign-in ≠ uploading calls

Google Sign-In is used for account authentication. Signing in does not mean Callio uploads your call log just to create an account.

You control SMS

Rescue messages go through your system SMS composer after you review them. Callio does not silently send SMS for you.

No SMS inbox reading

Callio does not read your SMS inbox. If you want help interpreting a reply, you provide or paste that reply yourself.

We avoid absolute claims like “100% private” or “zero data ever leaves your phone.” Supabase is used for authentication and identity — not as a dump of your call history.

Practical help for unknown missed calls

Useful reading for people searching what to do after a missed call from an unknown number — and where Callio fits.

What to do when you miss a call from an unknown number

First, pause. Unknown missed calls are common on Android, and not every one deserves an immediate call-back. Check whether the caller left voicemail. If the number looks familiar in context (delivery window, appointment day), that helps. Searching the number can sometimes provide clues — and sometimes it only adds noise.

Calling back immediately can work, but it can also connect you to spam, scams, or an awkward conversation with no context. Messaging first — when appropriate — can be a calmer way to ask why they called. Callio is built for that moment: it helps you start a careful “find out why” Rescue flow instead of guessing.

Should you call back an unknown number?

Sometimes yes — especially if you were expecting a doctor, school, delivery, or interview call. Sometimes no — especially if the pattern looks like robocalls or callback scams.

A practical middle path: gather context before you dial. If the caller can briefly explain why they called, you can decide whether a conversation is worth it. Callio supports that decision without pretending every unknown call is safe or every unknown call is spam.

How to find out who called you

Common approaches people try after an unknown missed call:

  • Caller ID labels (when available)
  • Voicemail
  • Searching the number online
  • Returning the call
  • Messaging the caller

Callio’s approach focuses less on naming the caller from a giant database and more on recovering the reason for the call through a user-confirmed message and the reply you choose to share with Callio.

How Callio Rescue works

1) Callio detects an unknown missed call.
2) You open Rescue and choose to find out why they called.
3) Callio prepares a message; you review it and send it yourself through the system SMS composer.
4) When the caller replies, you provide or paste the reply.
5) Callio helps interpret the response so you can decide what to do next.

Nothing in that flow requires Callio to auto-dial the caller or silently send SMS in the background.

Why people miss important calls

Busy meetings, Do Not Disturb, weak signal, childcare chaos, driving, and notification overload — important callers don’t always get through on the first try, and they don’t always leave voicemail. That’s why missed-call management matters: the cost of a blind call-back and the cost of ignoring a real need can both be high. Callio is meant to reduce that uncertainty.

Callio vs caller ID

Caller ID answers “who might this be?” Callio Rescue answers “why did they call?” Those questions overlap, but they’re not the same product job. Identifying a name can still leave you unsure what to do. Recovering the reason helps you choose whether to call back, wait, or ignore.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 23, 2026

Overview

Callio is a missed-call rescue assistant for Android. We design the product so call history and Rescue context remain local-first on your device whenever possible.

Account authentication

Callio may use Google Sign-In through Supabase Auth to authenticate your account. Authentication identity is separate from your private call history. Signing in does not mean Callio uploads your call log as a default cloud feature.

Call data & Rescue

Missed-call records, notes, and Rescue conversation context are designed to stay on your device. We do not sell call data and we do not operate Callio as a central caller-ID database.

SMS

Rescue SMS is user-confirmed through the system SMS composer. Callio does not read your SMS inbox and does not silently send messages in the current MVP.

Permissions

To detect unknown missed calls, Callio may request Android permissions such as call log / phone state and contacts (to distinguish known contacts). Permissions are used for product functionality.
